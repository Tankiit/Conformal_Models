## 🔍 Pilot read (appended 2026-11-11)

**First paired U-flip grid on the REAL official 3dshapes images** (480k HDF5; floor_level=0, u_levels=(1,8), n=4800, 600/class; partners are real counterfactual images — at ρ=1 they are outside the sampled set by construction).

**Machinery validated on real data:**
- Sanity (x,x) null = **exactly 0.000** in all 6 cells.
- Test-1 attribution textbook again: plain CBM T1 = **0.000 at both ρ** (no x-path; its ρ=1 leakage enters through the concept head), residual T1 = 0.006 → **0.928** (the residual channel is the x-path).
- Test-2 signal vs its ρ=0 null: **0.056 → 0.952** (cbm), **0.006 → 0.928** (residual), **0.002 → 0.915** (blackbox).

**De-saturation: the ρ=0 CBM cell is in target; shortcut cells saturate by design.**
- cbm @ ρ=0: acc **0.834**, set size **1.36** — non-trivial conformal sets (scale is genuinely hard through the bottleneck). This is the null cell and it is healthy.
- Every ρ=1 cell hits acc ≥ 0.997 — U = parity of Y is a perfect feature, so the shortcut regime saturates. Saturation at ρ=1 is expected and not a problem for the null-relative claim.
- residual/blackbox @ ρ=0 also saturate (1.000): the residual channel and the free backbone solve scale exactly, bypassing the bottleneck's discretized concept head. For the paper's contrast this is fine (the CBM is the deployed bottleneck under audit) but worth reporting explicitly.

**Step-3 gate still open:** at ρ=1 the probability-shift baseline separates as strongly (1.4–2.0) as the sets. One encouraging detail: cbm @ ρ=1 set-change **0.952 > flip rate 0.860** — ~9% of pairs change the set while the argmax does not flip. The "sets detect what probabilities miss" claim needs a partial-shortcut regime — **next run: ρ = 0.5 dose-response** (and/or concept-loss-weight sweep to soften the concept head).
